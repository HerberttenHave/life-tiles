# Worksheet: what goes into the life book?

Go through this worksheet before you start collecting photos. Ideally do it together with the person the book is for, or with brothers, sisters and other family. One hour of talking often gives more than a day of searching.

## 1. Who is it for?

- The name as it should appear in the app (for example "Mum", "Grandpa Jan" or the full name):
- Date and place of birth:
- What can this person still see and hear well? (large letters, reading aloud, few buttons)
- Who sets up the tablet and helps when something goes wrong?

## 2. Which chapters?

The app has eight fixed tiles, one per tab in the Excel file. You can give each tile its own name, and empty tiles disappear automatically.

| Tab | Default tile name | Your own name (optional) | What goes in? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Children | My children | | Per child: name, born, story, photos |
| Partner | My partner | for example the first name | Meeting, wedding, life together; possibly a farewell |
| Family | Children and grandchildren | | Each child's family, grandchildren |
| Homes | Where I lived | | Every address, with years |
| School | My schools | | Primary school, secondary school, training |
| Work | My work | | Jobs, volunteering, own business |
| Holidays | Holidays | | Trips, with place and year |
| Celebrations | Family and celebrations | | Parents, siblings, cousins, birthdays, anniversaries |

## 4. Stories

One to three short paragraphs per chapter. Good questions to bring stories back:

- What did you see when you looked out of the window?
- Who lived next door?
- How did you get to school, and who was your best friend?
- What did you earn in your first job, and what did you buy with it?
- Which holiday will you never forget, and why?
- Which party was the best one?

Write in the first or second person, whatever feels right for this person. Short is better: it has to be readable on a tablet.

## 5. Photos

- Where are they? (Google Photos, iCloud, the children's phones, photo albums, boxes)
- Who scans the old albums? (Google's PhotoScan app works well with a phone)
- Which people must be in it? (parents, siblings, grandchildren)
- Which photos should stay out? (passports, documents, photos someone dislikes)
- Should a chapter be in the app but not in the automatic slideshow? (for example a farewell)

## 6. Extras

- Old photos of the places themselves (the school, the street, the village). Try the local archive or Wikimedia Commons.
- Fixed questions per chapter with a short, checked answer that is read aloud. For example: "How many people lived in the village then?"
- Asking your own questions with the microphone (only with wifi, needs your own Google Cloud project; see step 9 of the guide).
